Quantmetrics-Lab is a research lab for quantitative finance. We collect market data, clean it,
fit models to it and report what the results do and do not support. These pages document how we
work - the data we start from, the models we reach for, the checks a result has to survive, and
the metrics we report it with.

This is methodology and platform documentation, not developer API documentation. Nothing here is
a recipe for a trade. Every figure is a worked example rather than a live number, and anything
that looks like a measurement is labelled as illustrative sample data.

## Who these pages are for

The docs are written for three readers and assume no more than one of them.

- A researcher who wants the reasoning behind a method, including its assumptions and failure
  modes.
- An analyst or trader who wants to reproduce a result and needs to know which knobs were turned.
- A newcomer who wants the vocabulary first and the mathematics second.

Every page is written so the first section stands alone. If you only read the opening paragraph of
a page, you should still know what the method is for.

## How to read them

The pages are ordered as a short course rather than as a reference list. Data sources and cleaning
comes first because every later result inherits its quality. The volatility modelling overview
introduces the family of models we use most. Backtesting and validation explains how a fitted model
earns trust. The risk metrics glossary defines the handful of numbers we report, and the glossary
collects the terms that appear across all of them.

Use the sidebar to move in reading order, or the list on the right to jump within a page. The
previous and next links at the foot of every page follow the same order as the sidebar.

## Scope and limits

Our work is research and education. It is not investment advice, it is not a trading signal, and
it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything. Where a method has a defensible use in a real
portfolio, we describe the method and leave the decision to the reader.

Three limits are worth stating plainly at the start, because they shape every page that follows.

1. Models are approximations. A good fit is not evidence that the underlying process is known; it
   is evidence that the approximation is locally useful.
2. Data is imperfect. Cleaning choices are judgements, and a different judgement can move a result.
3. Backtests are arguments, not proofs. A backtest shows that a claim is not obviously wrong, given
   the sample and the assumptions.
