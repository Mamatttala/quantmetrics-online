The problem with a volatility regime is never detection. It is detection *in time*.
On a chart covering two years, the shift is unmistakable: a flat stretch, then a
band that widens and stays wide. Standing inside that second week, the same
information is a handful of large moves, and the honest answer is that a handful
of large moves is also what a quiet regime occasionally produces.

## Two measures, two different questions

The most common reading of volatility is realised variance over a rolling window:

$$
\hat{\sigma}^2_t = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} r_{t-i}^2
$$

It answers a plain question: how large have recent returns been? The window length
decides the character of the answer. A short window reacts within days and is
noisy; a long window is smooth and slow, and it will keep reporting the old
regime for weeks after the shift has happened.

A GARCH(1,1) fit answers a different question. Writing `\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha \varepsilon_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2`,
the fitted `\alpha` carries the news and `\beta` carries the memory. The
persistence `\alpha + \beta` is the number that matters for a regime question,
because a shift in the *level* of volatility with unchanged persistence is a
different object from a shift in how long a shock lasts.

## The gap is the signal

In the illustrative sample, the short-window estimate moves first and the fitted
persistence follows a few weeks later. That ordering is the point: the level is
what a chart shows, and the persistence is what determines whether the new level
is a spell or a state.

Reading the two together turns an ambiguous question into a specific one. A
widening band with stable persistence is usually the same regime breathing
harder. A widening band with persistence moving toward one is a different
process, and the difference is measurable before it is obvious.

## What this is not

This is a description of a measurement gap, not a timing rule. Nothing here says
what to do about a regime, and the illustrative figures are a single synthetic
path - they are not evidence that the ordering holds in general. The useful claim
is narrow: if you only watch the level, you are discarding the parameter that
says how long the level will last.
