Almost everyone meets the unit-root test in the same role: a gate. Run it, read
the p-value, and if the series passes you may model the level; if it fails you
difference and move on. The verdict is binary and the statistic behind it is
discarded.

## The idea

Keep the statistic. Compute the test on overlapping windows through the sample
and carry the resulting series as a feature - standardised, lightly smoothed - as
one input among several. The motivation is that the *distance* from a unit root
changes slowly and continuously, while the verdict flips:

$$
\text{ADF}_t = f\left(y_{t-w+1}, \dots, y_t\right)
$$

For a stationary process the statistic sits well below its critical value; for a
process with a unit root it does not. Between those two, and over short windows,
it does both, depending on the window and the lag length chosen. That instability
is normally treated as a nuisance. Here it is the observation.

## Why it might be useful

Consider why the binary version is so fragile near the boundary. Adding one lag
changes the statistic; extending the window by a quarter changes it again; a
structural break late in the sample makes an `I(1)` series look stationary
before the break and nothing like it afterwards. Each of those is a fact about
the sample, not about the process, and the verdict compresses all of them into
one bit.

A slow-moving version of the statistic keeps that information. A signal that
depends on whether a relationship is stable should arguably be allowed to see
how stable the data says it is, rather than being switched on or off by a
threshold crossing.

## What to check before believing any of it

- Does the rolling statistic actually vary more than its own sampling noise? If
  not, there is nothing to carry.
- Is it collinear with features already in the model - realised volatility,
  autocorrelation, trend strength? If it is, it is a relabelling, not a new
  input.
- Does the lag length choice dominate the result? If it does, the feature is
  reporting the analyst's decision rather than the data.
- Does it survive out of sample? Most ideas of this shape do not, and the test
  has to be able to say so.

## Status

This is a proposal, not a finding. No result is claimed, no instrument is named,
and no action is suggested. It is written down because the asymmetry is
appealing: the gate discards a number, and the number is the part that moves.
